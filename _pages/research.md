*A short, rough sketch of my research directions and where I think they're heading.*

My work centers on **modeling and exploiting uncertainty to bridge representation gaps**. I began in computational imaging and efficient low-level vision, modeling mask/hardware uncertainty for more interpretable and robust reconstruction ([ECCV Oral](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/papers/136790109.pdf); [TPAMI](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10899382); [NeurIPS](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=zxSWIdyW3A)), and making super-resolution efficient via train-from-scratch pruning ([ICCV](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Wang_Iterative_Soft_Shrinkage_Learning_for_Efficient_Image_Super-Resolution_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf)). I then carried this perspective into the multimodal setting, studying and exploiting uncertainty across modalities: modeling text as a stochastic *"mass"* rather than a point for better text–video alignment ([T-MASS, CVPR Highlight](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Wang_Text_Is_MASS_Modeling_as_Stochastic_Embedding_for_Text-Video_Retrieval_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf)); recasting retrieval as diffusion-inspired iterative alignment ([NeurIPS](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=SrQua0ATRZ)) or as LLM chain-of-thought reasoning for explainable ranking ([X-CoT, EMNLP](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.21559)); and improving visual instruction via self-questioning ([SQ-LLaVA, ECCV](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.11299)). My guiding insight is that intelligent systems should *explore, address, and exploit* uncertainty (knowing, acting on, and benefiting from the unknown) for better performance, trustworthiness, and reduced hallucination.

Building on this, my current work explores three seemingly-separate directions that I firmly believe will ultimately come together (the **3 A's**): **Agent** — turning raw documents into controllable training environments for multimodal search/QA agents ([DocArena](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1HgomrTu4acObIiKn0u01xWaraJVs9JJW/view?usp=sharing), Adobe Research); **Algorithm** — post-training methods, including preference optimization and Bayesian-grounded approaches, that push environment signals into models efficiently and losslessly ([Visual Self-Refinement, EMNLP](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.00993); Visual Autoregressive Modeling, IROS); and **Autonomy** — post-training VLA models for autonomous driving (at NVIDIA). Interestingly, I see these three as equal partners rather than a hierarchy, and I often wonder how they might reinforce one another: the same post-training algorithms could push richer environment signals into both general agents and autonomous-driving models; autonomous driving, being embodied, real-time, and safety-critical, is in turn one of the most demanding environments for stress-testing and advancing agents; and the multi-turn, tool-using reasoning of agents could make driving systems more deliberate and trustworthy. What ties them together is a single thread: modeling and acting under uncertainty. This is simply how I like to think about my work, with new outcomes on the way in the coming months; I warmly welcome a chat on any of these directions, or the connections among them.
